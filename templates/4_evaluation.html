Summary

M1: Fleiss' Kappa (Inter-Model Agreement)

Measures agreement across all models simultaneously, correcting for chance. <0.2 = poor, 0.2–0.4 = fair, 0.4–0.6 = moderate, 0.6–0.8 = good, ≥0.8 = excellent. Low values suggest category boundaries are ambiguous — clarify descriptions and add hard negatives.

M2: Pairwise Agreement

How often each pair of models assigns the same top-1 category. Cohen's κ adjusts for chance. Low per-pair kappa points to specific model disagreements — sharpen overlapping category distinctions.

M3: Consensus Strength

Margin between top-voted and runner-up categories (confidence-weighted). High margins = clear-cut; low margins = boundary papers. Too many weak items suggests categories need merging or sharper differentiation.

M4: Confidence-Agreement Correlation

Pearson r between model confidence and actual agreement. High correlation means models “know when they know.” Low or negative r suggests category descriptions mislead models into false confidence.

M5: Per-Model Deviation

Fraction of papers where each model's top-1 pick differs from consensus. A consistently high rate flags a model as an outlier. Informational — consider excluding the outlier or investigating its disagreement patterns.

M6: Taxonomy Difficulty

Ranks taxonomies by how hard they are for models to agree on. Low unanimous rates and high entropy indicate a taxonomy is too fine-grained or has overlapping categories — consider merging similar leaves.

M7: Coverage Analysis (Structural Balance)

How papers distribute across leaf categories. Dead leaves (0 papers) and fat leaves (overcrowded) indicate structural imbalance. High Gini or low entropy means the taxonomy doesn't match the corpus — split fat leaves and merge/replace dead ones.

M8: Taxonomy Independence

Cramer's V measures whether taxonomy pairs classify papers differently. 0 = independent (good), 1 = redundant (one duplicates another). Redundant pairs should be merged or one replaced with a genuinely different perspective.
